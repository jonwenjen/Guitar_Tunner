- **Standard.** Fourths between every pair of strings except one major third (G–B). The compromise that makes scales and chord shapes fit under one hand; the reference every other tuning is described against. [weissman2006]
- **E♭ standard (half step down).** Standard shapes a semitone lower: slinkier strings for bends and a darker tone. Long associated with Jimi Hendrix, Stevie Ray Vaughan and Eddie Van Halen. [weissman2006]
- **D standard (whole step down).** Standard shapes a whole tone lower. Common in heavier rock and for singers who want songs a step down without relearning fingerings. [weissman2006]
- **C standard.** Standard shapes two whole tones down. Needs heavier strings (≈ .012–.056) to keep tension. [weissman2006]
- **Baritone (B standard).** A fourth below standard, the usual tuning for long-scale (27″–30″) baritone guitars. [weissman2006]
- **Nashville (high-strung).** The four wound strings are replaced by the thin octave strings of a 12-string set, so the guitar rings an octave up with standard fingerings. Used for shimmering doubled parts in studio work. [weissman2006]

- **Open E.** Strums an E major chord; same shapes as open D, a tone higher. A favourite for slide (Duane Allman). Raises three strings, so tension goes up. [weissman2006] [sethares]
- **Open D (Vestapol).** D major across the strings, called “Vestapol” by early blues players. Lower tension than open E, deep and resonant for slide and fingerstyle. [weissman2006] [sethares]
- **Open G (Spanish / Taropatch).** G major with the root on the 5th string. Called “Spanish” in Delta blues and “Taropatch” in Hawaiian slack key; Keith Richards plays it with the 6th string removed. [weissman2006] [sethares] [winston]
- **Open A.** Open G shapes a tone higher. High tension; many players capo open G at the 2nd fret instead. [weissman2006] [sethares]
- **Open C.** A huge, low C major chord. Needs a heavier 6th string; the 5th and 6th strings drop a long way. [sethares]
- **Open D minor (cross-note).** Open D with the third lowered to F. The “cross-note” tuning of Skip James’ haunting Bentonia blues. [weissman2006] [sethares]
- **Open G minor.** Open G with the B lowered to B♭. [sethares]

- **All fourths.** Standard with the G–B third removed: every chord and scale shape is identical on every string set, like a bass guitar. Used by Stanley Jordan. [sethares] [griewank2010]
- **Major thirds.** Every string a major third apart, so the pattern repeats every three strings and chords need at most four frets. Pioneered by Ralph Patt in 1964; Griewank (2010) argues it makes reading music on the fretboard simpler, and Anderson et al. (2015) found novices learned chords faster on a major-thirds grid. [griewank2010] [anderson2015] [sethares]
- **All fifths (mandoguitar).** Fifths like a mandolin, violin or cello: wide range from C2 to B4, but the top B4 needs a very light string. [sethares] [griewank2010]
- **New Standard Tuning (NST).** Robert Fripp’s 1983 tuning, taught through Guitar Craft: all fifths with the top string lowered to G to avoid breakage, giving open-voiced fifths and a minor third on top. [tamm1990] [sethares]
- **Augmented fourths.** Tritones between every string; only two pitch classes open. A theoretical curiosity that repeats every two strings. [sethares]
